Working within the Art Department on House of David, I was involved in creating
and dressing the physical world of the series, set around 1000 BCE. My role
moved from prop sourcing and fabrication through to the final dressing of sets,
helping transform contemporary locations and builds into environments that felt
lived-in, historically grounded and visually rich.

The work ranged from densely layered marketplaces, trading goods and domestic
spaces to ceremonial and royal interiors, using textiles, aged surfaces, props
and set dressing to establish the character and atmosphere of each environment.
Alongside sourcing and dressing, I worked across scenic treatments, extensive
fabric dyeing and graphic elements, developing symbols and iconography for
flags, coins and other details appearing throughout the story. I also designed
Saul's throne, taking the piece from design through to fabrication.

Period pieces particularly interests me because of its constraints. Creating a
convincing historical world requires thinking beyond contemporary materials and
objects, and finding inventive ways to communicate status, culture and everyday
life through what could plausibly have existed at the time.
