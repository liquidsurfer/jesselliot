During a two-week artist residency in Woodstock, Cape Town, I was given the
freedom to develop a new body of work through unrestricted material and visual
experimentation. The residency culminated in a series of 12 original works,
exhibited in the space at the end of the programme.

Working directly onto unprimed, unstretched raw canvas, I explored the
relationship between painting and textile through layers of pigment, hand-dyed
fabric, netting, hessian and sewn elements. Rather than treating the canvas
simply as a surface, I began cutting, layering, gathering and attaching
materials so that the construction of each piece became part of the image
itself.

The resulting works move between organic and architectural forms, softness and
structure, transparency and density. Repeated lines, blocks and fluid shapes
create an abstract visual language, while exposed edges, stitching and layered
textiles retain the physicality and imperfection of the making process.

The residency became an opportunity to work intuitively and at pace, allowing
one experiment to inform the next and developing a material language that could
move freely between painting, textile and installation.

I encouraged the outcome of these works not to stand as conventional framed
"paintings." I wanted them to hang and fray in their layers, illuminating the
draped netting and raw edges by the way the canvases hang off the wall allowing
them to speak more as textile objects existing in the space as a soft
installation.
