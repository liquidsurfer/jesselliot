Die Suurlemoen Padstal Pop Up stage at Wolfkop Festival reimagined the
traditional South African padstal: the characterful roadside farm stalls found
throughout the Western Cape. Through a playful festival lens with Brazilian and
Spanish influences. Drawing from their eclectic mix of local produce,
hand-painted signage, nostalgic objects and collected trinkets, we created a
small world that felt familiar, slightly surreal and full of character.

The name itself was a play on words and tribute to Afrikaans culture that
Wolfkop celebrates. Suurlemoen translates literally to "sour lemon" in
Afrikaans, but actually means orange.

This pop up instillation became a playful, interactive roadside bar and
micro-stage created for Wolfkop Festival. A shaded pit stop on the journey
towards the main dancefloor, offering fresh orange juice, Paloma's music,
performance and a space to slow down and gather.

Nestled beneath the trees, the space was built through scenic painting, found
and fabricated décor, signage and layered details, transforming an otherwise
transitional area into a destination of its own. By day it operated as a
relaxed roadside stop; by night, lighting, music and spontaneous performances
shifted it into a lively little world of its own.

The project was an exploration of how even a small-scale intervention can
create a strong sense of place, using humour, cultural references and immersive
detail to invite people to stop, interact and become part of the environment.
